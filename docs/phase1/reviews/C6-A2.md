## Outcome: accepted

Nothing in C6 misreads a device record. The findings below are for the parser (A4, Phase 2), for my C2, and for A6's simulator. They are recorded here because C6's renderings are where they would first show.

## Checked and agreed

- **Escaping (RFC 5424 section 6.3.3).** `"`, `\` and `]` are escaped in every PARAM-VALUE. Names are fixed ASCII tokens, at most 13 characters, well inside the 32 allowed. Device bytes appear only in `dtext` or `dtext_b64`, never in MSG or in the header.
- **Header time.** HEADER TIMESTAMP is the node's receive time. It is cut to 6 digits, and `rcv` carries the full value. The device time travels separately (`dt`, `dwall`, `doff`). This is what the RTAC requires: its own header time is the send time, 9.4 s from the event time in S23's example (`A2-q5-rtac.md`). C6 never promotes either RTAC time to the forwarded header.
- **Nested Syslog.** The RTAC's own message, `<190>` and its header included, sits escaped inside `dtext`. An upstream collector cannot mistake it for the forwarded message's PRI or header.
- **Unknown offset.** `doff="unknown"` with `dbasis="none"` for both the SEL-751 and the RTAC, and no `dutc`. This matches both manuals: the SEL-751 has `UTC_OFF` only with SNTP, and S23 states no zone for the tag time.
- **RELP inbound grammar.** `TXNR SP COMMAND SP DATALEN [SP DATA] LF`, with `serverclose` at TXNR 0 and `rsp` only for an outstanding transaction, matches RELP as librelp implements it. Bounding DATALEN to 4 digits and 4,096 octets is stricter than the protocol (9 digits), and fine for responses.

## Findings (none blocking C6)

| # | Finding | Evidence | For |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **SEL-751 frames every message with STX and ETX.** "The relay transmits all messages in the following format: `<STX><MESSAGE LINE 1><CRLF>` … `<LAST MESSAGE LINE><CRLF><ETX>`". ASCII 02 opens each message and ASCII 03 closes it. None of our fixtures, C2 examples or simulator outputs carry them. | S22 p. 7.28 | **A2 (C2):** the transcript keeps them. Prompt detection and `ProbeReplyAccepted` must allow `0x02` before the first line and `0x03` after the last line end. I will add them to the C2 examples. **A4 (parser):** a record's bytes are the row text without STX, ETX or CRLF. If one leaked in, C6 would send the row as `dtext_b64` (safe, but unreadable) and `rsha256` would change. **A6:** the simulator should emit both. H-12 should confirm them on Telnet. The manual states it for all messages, but no capture exists. |
| 2 | **The SER date order is a setting.** `DATE_F` is MDY, YMD or DMY, and the SER and report header dates follow it. The goldens carry `dflag="date-format-assumed:MDY"`, which is correct when the setting was not read. But a DMY relay read as MDY swaps day and month on every date with a day of 12 or less, and the result still looks valid. | S22 Table 4.90 p. 4.225 (`DATE_F := MDY` default), p. 7.31 ("You can modify the date display format … by changing the DATE_F relay setting"); C3 `sel.settings.show_global` already cites `DATE_F` | **A4:** the parser takes `DATE_F` from the `SHO G` read in the same session (the register already allows it). The "assumed" flag is then only for sessions without that read. A test should parse one row as MDY and as DMY. |
| 3 | **The RFC 3164 header carries UTC with no zone and no year.** `time.Stamp` gives `Oct 24 12:00:05`. RFC 3164 receivers conventionally take it as local time, and the year is guessed at a year boundary. `rcv` is unaffected. | `render.go` `assemble`; RFC 3164 section 4.1.2 | **A4:** one sentence in `doc.go` saying that the 3164 header time is UTC receive time without year, and that `rcv` is authoritative. |
| 4 | **The RTAC golden freezes a manual artefact.** `subj="SystemTags. User_Logged_On"` keeps the space after the dot. My fixture's `SOURCE.md` says that space is as printed in S23 and may not be on the wire. | `tests/fixtures/ied/sel/rtac/documented-example/SOURCE.md` | **A4:** NOTES.md should say the RTAC rendering is built from a documented example, so its `subj` is not evidence of the parser's output on real RTAC traffic. |
| 5 | **RTAC messages near 1,024 bytes.** The RTAC sends up to 1,024 bytes (S23 p. 635). On a 3164 target, capped at 1,024, the escaped `dtext` plus the SD element will not fit. Every long RTAC message then forwards with `dtext_omitted="size"`. That is the designed behaviour, and the store keeps the bytes. | S23 p. 635; `MaxOctets3164` | Informational. A site forwarding RTAC Syslog upstream should use an RFC 5424 target of at least 2,048 octets. |
