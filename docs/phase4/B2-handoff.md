## What the read does

One session, one `ACC`, 24 `SHO` forms in the register's order (`SHO P 1` first so `LANG := ENGLISH` is confirmed before any other line, then `SHO G`, then the rest), then `QUI`. Each step has its own timeout (10 s) and reply cap from the register, and the run has a 512 KiB total and a 300 s deadline (`Limits.ForRun`). The result is exactly one of two shapes (checked by `Result.CheckLines`):

- **ok**: every step sent once and in order, and one parsed record per step in step order, `sel.config.section` or `sel.config.absent`.
- **not ok**: a prefix of the steps and no record. The bytes are kept. `partial` is not a run outcome, and a record that did not parse is not a valid configuration record.

Failures of the run as a whole: `failed.timeout`, `failed.response_too_large`, `failed.access_level_dropped` (no `QUI`, nothing to quit), `failed.unexpected_access_level` (`QUI`), a password prompt (no `QUI`), `failed.device_error`, and the new `failed.config_unparsable` for a reply C9's rule cannot read. A gate form (`SHO P 1`, `SHO G`) answering `Invalid Command` is `failed.device_error`, checked before the language check, so it does not suspend the asset. Only the eleven forms C9 marks `MayBeAbsent` (ports 2 to 4, Modbus, IEC 103, EtherNet/IP, DNP) can answer `Invalid Command` and become `sel.config.absent`.

`config.FromResult` builds a C9 snapshot from an ok run: the settings the adapter's parser read, the SHA-256 of each span, and no tokenizing of its own. `config.Compare` gives the semantic difference, with `ChangesDigest` (compact JSON of the changes array, as C8 0.7 cites; `[]` for none, equal to `audit.EmptyChangesDigest`) and `SameChanges`.

## Interfaces

- **Record size against the 1.5 MiB cap (simulated).** C9 0.3 (D4-25) bounds a snapshot at 10,918 settings (S22's 8,734 plus a quarter) and 1 MiB encoded, and the adapter ends a run over either as `failed.config_unparsable` (transcript kept, no record, no snapshot). With S22's 8,734 settings and values filling the run's 512 KiB limit, the record (snapshot plus transcript) is 1,210,476 bytes, 77% of the cap. At the 10,918 bound it is 1,251,954 bytes, 79.6%. At both bounds with a full transcript it is exactly 1.5 MiB (`TestBoundsFitTheCap`). Pinned by `TestConfigMaximalRecordFitsTheCap`, `TestConfigOverBoundRunFails` and the C9 tests `TestSizeBounds`, `TestCountBoundIsExact` and `TestRecordsFitImpliesValidate`.
- The largest ok result is now 1,428,686 bytes of JSON (`TestConfigMaximalResultFrame`), about 17% of the 8 MiB outcome frame. The 2,472,562-byte figure in #338 was for a read that is now refused.

## Limitations

- **Touchscreen settings are not read and not baselined** (owner, 2026-10-28). The model is in scope. The 175 `/HMI` settings (S22 Table 7.80 p. 7.87) have no `SHO` form, and a change in them is not seen. No document may claim the snapshot covers them.
- No FTP, SNMP or Level 2 read, and no write of any kind.
- Reply sizes and times are bounds from the settings sheets, not measurements. The record layouts in the goldens are invented; C9's own goldens carry S22's printed layouts.
- The SER rows "Relay Powered Up" and "Relay Settings Changed" stay deferred until an H-13 capture shows how a relay prints them.

## Risks

- A real relay may print a section in a form the parser does not read. The run then fails with `failed.config_unparsable` and no snapshot, which is safe but means no baseline for that relay until the parser is fixed from a capture. The bytes are kept for that.
- The run holds one session for up to 300 s. Whether a lab relay tolerates 24 `SHO` forms in a row with `TIDLE` at its default is unmeasured (H-13).
- `gofmt` in the container's `/usr/local/go/bin` is older than the module's Go; use `$(go env GOROOT)/bin/gofmt -l .`.
