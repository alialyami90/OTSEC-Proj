# C5 0.5 proposal: the WAL a commit writes counts its rows (P4-A5, D4-14)

Owner: B5. Date: 2026-10-27. Status: **contract change for B1's approval; B6 writes the contract tests.** Every number here is `simulated` (Linux, modernc SQLite) until B6's storekill leg runs.

## Why

C5 0.4's reserve rule, `2 × WALBoundBytes + 1.1 × C + 64 KiB`, assumed a commit's WAL is about its payload. B6's event-size sweep (#280) showed it is not: `events` is a `WITHOUT ROWID` table, so each event over about 1 KB spills to an overflow page of its own. One SER read of 2048 events of 5.3 KB (11.47 MB of payload) wrote 19.31 MB of WAL against a rule of 12.68 MB, and the checkpointed file grew 4.07 to 4.53 times the bytes the quota counts (D4-9 item 6). The rule must also count rows, and the quota-to-disk gap must be a stated bound.

## What changes

1. **`GroupOptions.MaxCommitRecords`** (0 means `MaxEvents`, at least `MaxEvents`, at most `MaxBatch`) bounds the records in one commit as `MaxCommitBytes` bounds its bytes. An `AppendBatch` of more records is refused whole with `CodeRecordTooLarge`. The producer splits it, and a receiver splits at the byte cap and the record cap alike.
2. **`CommitWALBytes(payload, records)`** is `1.1 × payload + 64 KiB + RowWALBytes × records`. `RowWALBytes` is **3 KiB**: the worst excess over the 0.4 rule in B6's sweep is 2,051 bytes a record (128 events of 2 KB, which take a leaf page alone on a rowid table), plus about 50%.
3. **`RecoveryReserveFor(walBound, largestCommit, records)`**, **`LargestCommitRecords(d, opt)`** (one for a database that holds only the configuration class, whose commit is one record and the small holds it takes; otherwise the record cap) and **`StartupPolicy.LargestCommitRecords`**. A policy sets the WAL bound, the largest commit and its records together or not at all.
4. **`DiskBytesPerQuotaByte` = 2.5.** Once checkpointed, a database file grows by at most this many bytes per byte its class quotas count. Measured worst case 2.02, at events of 2 KB on a rowid table (two such events do not fit one page); 2.5 leaves 24%. Volume sizing is `sum of class quotas × 2.5 + each database's recovery and last-resort reserves`, and no volume figure may come from raw quota sums.
5. **`MaxHoldsPerRecord`** = 16: a record takes at most this many holds (a held difference holds two snapshots and itself, so three), which keeps a configuration commit's rows inside the fixed term.
6. **`Identifier`** becomes `otsec.store/0.5`.

## Layout and old databases (the next PR, stacked on this one)

- `events` and `captures` become rowid tables (`id INTEGER PRIMARY KEY`) with `UNIQUE INDEX (stream, seq)` and `UNIQUE INDEX (sha256)`. Every query is by those keys, so no SQL changes. Rows up to about 4 KB stay in the leaf. `captures` was left `WITHOUT ROWID` in the first draft of the layout PR; B6 found that commits where every record has its own 1 to 1.5 KB capture wrote 3.9 KB of WAL a record (1.16 × the rule) and grew the file 3.48 ×. `TestFileGrowthWithACaptureEachStaysUnderTheDiskFactor` reproduces 3.92 × on the old table and measures 1.34 to 2.00 × on the rowid one.
- A database created by earlier code is **refused at open** with a clear error and the `store-open-failed` alarm, and is never deleted or rewritten. The check reads the format row from the main file alone and immutable, before any connection is opened, so a store left with a hot `-wal` is refused with its file, `-wal` and `-shm` byte for byte as found (an ordinary open folds the `-wal` in and deletes it; the first draft refused later, at the settings step with `store-settings-invalid`, and did that). The error names the way out: export the stream through Syslog forwarding or `otsec` export, remove the store directory, and start again. There is no migration: it needs free space equal to the database, and all data so far is simulated.
- Tests: `#280` against the new rule; a 100 B to 64 KiB sweep at 1 and 2048 rows; a pin that the measured file growth stays under `DiskBytesPerQuotaByte` × the quota bytes.

## Confirmed on the built layout (layout PR, B6's #280 tests merged onto it)

All `simulated`, Linux, modernc SQLite, 4 KiB pages.

- B6's six SER-read shapes and the 89-size sweep (100 B to 64 KiB, 128 events a commit) pass against `CommitWALBytes(payload, records)` with `RowWALBytes` 3 KiB: 0 of 89 sizes over the rule. The worst excess is **2,051 bytes a record**, at events of 2,048 bytes, 70% of the term. The worst file growth is **2.02 ×** the bytes the quota counts, also at 2,048 bytes, 81% of `DiskBytesPerQuotaByte`.
- The 2048-row case, which the sweep's 128 rows do not reach: 2048 events of 1,900, 2,048, 2,200 and 2,600 bytes in one commit with a 145 KB cursor. The worst wrote 9,158,760 bytes of WAL for a 4.27 MB payload (2,148 bytes a record beyond `1.1 × payload + 64 KiB`, 70% of the term), against a rule of 11.05 MB. The term holds at 2048 rows.
- One configuration record with `MaxHoldsPerRecord` (16) holds, at 64 KiB, 512 KiB and 1.5 MiB: 107,152, 568,592 and 1,623,312 bytes of WAL against rules of 140,576, 645,203 and 1,798,637. The 16 holds add 4,120 bytes (one page) over the same record with none.
- The file-to-quota ratio over 300 B to 64 KiB events in the store's own tests (`TestFileGrowthStaysUnderTheDiskFactor`): 1.22, 1.36, 1.09, 2.03, 1.73, 1.38, 1.06 and 1.02.

## Alternative measured and not taken: smaller pages

Rowid `events`, B6's 89-size sweep (128 events a commit), page size set before the first table:

| Page size | Sizes over the 0.4 rule | Worst WAL ÷ rule | Worst file ÷ quota |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4096 (today) | 53 | 1.72 | 2.02 at 2048 B |
| 2048 | 28 | 1.54 | 2.12 at 1024 B |
| 1024 | 15 | 1.28 | 2.19 at 512 B |
| 512 | 10 | 1.03 (excess 5.7 KB) | 1.76 at 256 B |

512-byte pages would shrink the per-record term from 3 KiB to about 0.25 KiB and the evidence reserve by about 5 MiB. Not taken (B1): deeper trees, more WAL frames, and a WAL bound that would have to be re-derived are more risk than that disk is worth. B6 measured 16 KiB and 64 KiB pages and they fail the fixed term more often.

## Limits

- The per-record term and the factor are measured on a synthetic event shape (`serEvent`, hex padding) and on random capture bytes. Real SER events are 1.7 to 2 KB, near the worst case. Re-measure on the built layout (done in the layout PR) and on site hardware.
- The reserves stay `desk` until B6's storekill leg shows recovery at a full volume.
